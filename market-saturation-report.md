# Market Saturation Analysis — "The Undercroft"

*Deep-research run: 2026-07-04. 107 research agents, 24 sources fetched, 25 claims adversarially verified (20 confirmed, 5 refuted). Popularity figures dated to that date; Steam numbers drift. Estimates labeled as such.*

---

## 1. Concept decomposition

The spec breaks into eight comparable dimensions. Candidates were scored against these in priority order (core loop first, visual style last):

| Dimension | The Undercroft's value |
|---|---|
| **Core loop** | Extraction/gathering expeditions from a hub → bank loot or lose it on death |
| **Primary mechanic** | Light as a *single unified economy* — HP + weapon + timer + currency + territory |
| **Combat model** | No conventional weapons; light staggers/repels; evasion is the verb |
| **Genre/subgenre** | Single-player stealth-evasion survival horror |
| **Perspective** | First-person (dread-maximizing) |
| **Progression structure** | Persistent buildable hub ("Last Lantern") + metroidvania revisits + light-tech depth gating |
| **Tone/theme** | Oppressive underground ruin, dark-vs-light, DS1 Catacombs register |

| **Unique hooks** | (a) carried light *attracts* / planted light *repels*; (b) hub brightness *is* the progress meter; (c) one resource starves all its other uses |

---

## 2. Saturation verdict

**The exact fusion is white space; every individual dimension is contested.** The research found **no verified released game that combines all four pillars** — single-player + first-person + no-combat light-as-weapon + hub-rebuilding + extraction stakes. The closest relatives split into clean camps that each own one or two pillars and cede the rest. That is the central finding: The Undercroft is not entering a crowded field. It is landing in a gap bordered by strong titles on each side.

**Where the density actually is.** The mechanic clusters have real occupants, just never assembled together:

1. *Light-as-weapon extraction* exists — Level Zero: Extraction — but only as PvPvE multiplayer with firearms.
2. *No-combat first-person light-as-resource horror* is mature and commercially proven — the entire Amnesia line plus the Slender→Alien: Isolation stalker lineage — but it is linear, with no hub and no extraction.
3. *Hub-rebuild + expedition meta-loops* are proven — Against the Storm (95%, 18.5k reviews) — but as top-down management with zero horror, stealth, or light economy.
4. *Survival-horror scavenging with light-as-safety* is commercially validated — Darkwood (est. 1–2M owners) — but top-down and procedural.

Each border is well-defended; the interior is empty.

**The exploitable gaps.** Three of your hooks are genuinely differentiated, not derivative:

- **"Carried light attracts hunters"** is *not* the Amnesia cliché people assume. The widely-repeated claim that light attracts monsters in *Amnesia: The Dark Descent* was **refuted 0-3** in verification (it's a persistent myth; DD's darkness is a *sanity* mechanic, not a lure). Only *Amnesia: The Bunker* (2023) actually implements light-attracts-monster, and it's linear with no hub. So attract/repel light as a *spatial economy* is close to unoccupied.
- **"Planted light claims safe territory"** (light as spatial control you deploy) appears in **none** of the verified comparables.
- **"Hub brightness as the literal progress meter"** has no verified analog.

The caution: the two pillars that *never co-occur* in the entire verified set are "persistent buildable hub" + "first-person extraction loop." That vacancy could mean underserved — or it could mean prior attempts failed and delisted. The research couldn't confirm a successful precedent for that specific pairing, which is a risk flag as much as an opportunity.

## 4. Per-game analysis

### 1. Level Zero: Extraction — *the closest core-loop match*
- **Why it's similar:** The single most on-point find for the light-as-economy pillar. Flashlights, UV lamps, and flares repel aliens *and reveal your position* — the exact attract/repel tension you're centering. First-person extraction loop with survival-horror framing. Matches: core loop (extraction), light-as-weapon, FP, horror tone.
- **Where it differs:** Fundamentally **PvPvE multiplayer** (rival mercenaries + player-or-AI aliens) and light only *damages aliens* while **conventional firearms** do the human-vs-human work. Breaks your single-player and no-weapons locks. Procedural, not handcrafted.
- **Market performance:** Released 2024, actively updated; exact counts not pinned here (see open questions — worth checking its SteamDB CCU directly, as it's your most direct demand signal for light-as-weapon extraction).
- **Takeaway:** Proof the light-repel + extraction fantasy resonates enough to ship commercially — but its multiplayer/gun framing is exactly the space you're *avoiding*. Your single-player, no-gun version is a genuine differentiation, not a me-too.

### 2. Amnesia: The Bunker — *the closest mechanic match on light-attracts*
- **Why it's similar:** Semi-open FP horror where **fuel is a survival resource** and using light (pulling the generator/flashlight cord) **attracts the Beast**. The one verified game that implements your "carried light is a beacon" hook. Matches: FP dread, no-combat evasion, light-as-depleting-resource, active hunt.
- **Where it differs:** Linear-ish single location, no settlement hub, no extraction/bank-it economy, no hub-growth motivator. Light is a liability to ration, not a currency you also spend on building.
- **Market performance:** 2023, Frictional's strongest-reviewed entry in years, "Very Positive."
- **Takeaway:** Validates that light-attracts-monster is *fun and shippable* — and that Frictional got there in 2023, so it's fresh in the genre's memory. Your edge is wrapping it in an economy (attract/repel/build/reveal) rather than a single survival axis.

### 3. Darkwood — *the commercial proof for dark-scavenging*
- **Why it's similar:** Scavenge a hostile dark world, retreat to a hideout, light provides safety. Directly matches your dread-scavenging loop and light-as-safety pillar; also proves an *oppressive, non-jumpscare* darkness horror sells.
- **Where it differs:** **Top-down**, not FP (kills the Amnesia-register dread you're after); procedural, not handcrafted zones; survival-crafting rather than extraction-with-persistent-hub-growth.
- **Market performance:** **SteamSpy est. 1M–2M owners; 94% of 10,023 Steam reviews positive** (SteamDB: 94.9% of 22,352). Still sold, still praised as a cult benchmark. *(Owner band is a wide SteamSpy estimate.)*
- **Takeaway:** The strongest evidence your *tone* has a paying audience. The lesson is that its top-down camera capped its dread ceiling — your FP choice is the upgrade path, if the voxel lighting delivers.

### 4. Amnesia: The Dark Descent — *the genre-founder for your combat model*
- **Why it's similar:** The template for no-weapons FP horror with a depleting light resource (tinderbox/oil) forcing run-or-hide. Matches your combat model and light-as-resource pillars almost exactly.
- **Where it differs:** Linear narrative, no hub, no extraction, no building. And critically — **its light does NOT attract monsters** (that verified-false myth means your attract mechanic is *not* copied from the genre's most famous example).
- **Market performance:** 2010, "Overwhelmingly Positive," revived the entire combat-free horror subgenre; still the reference point 15 years on.
- **Takeaway:** Establishes your combat model as proven and beloved, not risky. You're extending a validated core (no-combat light horror) with structure it never had (hub + extraction).

### 5. Darkest Dungeon — *the light-economy + hub-rebuild structural twin*
- **Why it's similar:** The clearest precedent for **light as explicit risk/reward** — bright torch (Radiant) gives safety/dodge; letting it burn low raises loot/gold draw but adds stress and Shambler-ambush risk at zero light. Plus a rebuild-the-Hamlet hub and an expedition loop. Matches your light-economy *and* hub-expedition structure — two pillars at once.
- **Where it differs:** Top-down **turn-based party RPG**, not FP real-time evasion. Light is one meter among many, not the unified spine.
- **Market performance:** 2016, ~150k+ reviews "Very Positive," a genre benchmark with a successful sequel.
- **Takeaway:** Direct proof that "torch as tension dial" + "hub you rebuild between runs" is a durable, commercial combination. You're porting that proven *structure* into FP dread — the transplant nobody has verified as done.

### 6. Alien: Isolation — *the stalker-AI benchmark for your hunt register*
- **Why it's similar:** The gold standard for FP evasion of an **undefeatable stalker** with advanced AI — exactly the "being hunted in the dark is the core experience" pillar (Amnesia/Alien register you named yourself).
- **Where it differs:** Sci-fi, linear, no hub, no light-economy, no extraction. Combat de-emphasized but not absent.
- **Market performance:** 2014, ~100k+ reviews "Very Positive," still cited as the best stalker AI ever shipped.
- **Takeaway:** Your active-hunt pillar has a genre ceiling to aim at. Also a warning: its AI is *expensive*. Your light-attract/repel system is partly an elegant, cheaper substitute for that AI sophistication — hunters keyed to your light state rather than needing Isolation-grade behavior trees.

### 7. Against the Storm — *the hub-meta-progression match*
- **Why it's similar:** Persistent hub (the Smoldering City) carries resources, upgrades, and experience across discrete expedition runs — structurally your hub-growth-between-expeditions loop.
- **Where it differs:** Top-down roguelite **city-builder**; no FP, no horror, no stealth, no light. Overlaps on the meta-structure axis *only*.
- **Market performance:** **18,546 reviews, 95% positive** (verified 2026-07-04); a breakout management hit, actively updated.
- **Takeaway:** Confirms strong appetite for "persistent hub feeding roguelite-ish expeditions." It owns the management flavor of that loop; the horror-extraction flavor is wide open — that's your lane.

### 8. Lethal Company — *the demand-ceiling proof for hub+extraction horror*
- **Why it's similar:** Hub-ship + extraction/quota horror loop. Proves enormous appetite for "leave a safe base, grab loot in a hostile dark, extract or die."
- **Where it differs:** **Co-op** and **procedural** — the inverse of your single-player, handcrafted spec. The tension is social/comedic, not lonely dread.
- **Market performance:** **All-time peak 240,817 concurrent players (3 Dec 2023, SteamDB); ~508k reviews ~96%.** A phenomenon.
- **Takeaway:** The extraction-horror *appetite* is proven at massive scale. The open question your concept bets on: how much of that demand survives the removal of co-op? The 240k peak was substantially a social phenomenon — don't assume it transfers 1:1 to solo dread.

## 5. Notable near-misses (the boundary of the set)

- **Retreat From Darkness** — thematically dead-on (extraction, darkness) but **unreleased, zero reviews**; fails the released/popularity floor.
- **Expedition: Into Darkness** — on-theme but **not yet released** (planned Q4 2026, date repeatedly slipped), zero reviews.
- **Deep Dark Dungeon of Anglaus** — FP dungeon horror but **~50 reviews (~40× below the floor)** and makes **sword combat a core verb** with no hub/extraction.
- **Sunless Sea / Subnautica** — your own comparables; excluded from finalists because the *camera + genre frame* (top-down naval / open-world crafting) put them below the mechanic-priority bar despite strong resource-as-lifeline theming.
- **Hollow Knight** — reviving hub + rescued NPCs + underground melancholy, but 2D combat metroidvania; theme/structure match only, core loop diverges hard.

The fact that the *three* most on-the-nose title matches are all either unreleased or sub-floor is itself a saturation signal: the specific niche is **nascent, not crowded** — people are *starting* to aim here, but nobody popular has landed it yet.

---

## Bottom line

This is an **underserved fusion inside a set of well-defended borders**. Your three differentiators — attract/repel light as spatial economy, planted-light-as-territory, hub-brightness-as-progress-meter — survived verification as genuinely uncommon (the attract mechanic notably *not* being the Amnesia cliché people will assume).

Two flags to weigh:

1. The hub + first-person-extraction pairing has **no verified successful precedent**, so you're pioneering rather than iterating on that axis.
2. Lethal Company's scale was partly social — solo-dread demand is real (Amnesia, Darkwood, Alien: Isolation prove it) but more modest than the co-op numbers suggest. Scope and price accordingly to the **Darkwood/Amnesia tier, not the Lethal Company tier**.

## Methodology & caveats

- **Verification:** 78 claims extracted → top 25 verified via 3-vote adversarial checking (2/3 refutes to kill). 20 confirmed, 5 refuted.
- **Notable refutation:** The claim that light attracts monsters in *Amnesia: The Dark Descent* was refuted 0-3 — it's a persistent community myth. This *strengthens* your differentiation, since the attract mechanic isn't derivative of the genre's most-cited example.
- **Estimate flags:** Darkwood owner counts (1M–2M) are a wide SteamSpy estimate; post-2018 API restrictions reduced SteamSpy accuracy. Lethal Company popularity rests on SteamDB peak-CCU. Several mechanical/atmospheric claims originated in blog roundups (GameRant, TheGamer, Bloody-Disgusting) but were corroborated against primary sources (official wikis, Wikipedia, Game Developer).
- **Time sensitivity:** All Steam store data fetched 2026-07-04. Review counts and unreleased-title statuses (esp. Expedition: Into Darkness) will change.
